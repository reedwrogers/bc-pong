bc-pong

Behavioral Cloning Pong

arrow keys to move  ·  space to pause AI  ·  first to 5 wins

Why?

I watched the AlphaGo documentary and was fascinated by the idea: you start with a model that's just somewhat competent, then let it play against itself thousands of times and it comes out the other side surprisingly strong. I wanted to understand that whole pipeline end-to-end on a tiny scale.

How It Works

  • Step 1 - Data Collection: My wife and I played Pong against each other while every frame was logged to CSV.
  • Step 2 - Behavioral Cloning: A neural network was trained to predict human paddle actions from frame state, basically mimicking us.
  • Step 3 - Self-Play: The cloned model played against itself (64 parallel games, 5,000 total), nudging its weights toward winning rather than just imitating.

The Model

MLP 10 → 96 → 96 → 3 ReLU + Dropout 10,659 params

I had an LLM pick the architecture and feature set. I didn't think too hard about the model choice. The point was the journey from behavioral cloning to self-play, not hyperparameter tuning. My focus was on learning how the full pipeline fits together: data collection, supervised cloning, reinforcement fine-tuning, then browser inference. The model is small enough that forward passes run in vanilla JS right here in the browser.

What's Next

This model is purely reactive (one frame at a time). A natural next step would be stacking frames to give it a sense of velocity and trajectory over time. Could also experiment with a PPO-based approach, add a value head for better credit assignment, or train against a wider variety of play styles.
